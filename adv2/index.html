Forgetting Names And Faces? What May Be Behind Everyday Memory Slips After 50

An educational look at the popular “Vicks trick”, at blood flow and the brain, and at a food supplement that people are asking about.

Sponsored content from The Living Dispatch · Updated 29 September 2026

Walking into a room and forgetting why. Struggling to bring a familiar name to mind. Putting the keys down and not finding them ten minutes later. If this sounds familiar, you are far from alone. Many adults over 50 notice that recall is not quite as quick as it used to be.

Occasional slips are common and are often linked to everyday factors such as poor sleep, stress, busy schedules or simply getting older. This article looks at what is commonly discussed about the topic, and what people can do to look after their brain health.

Is it just age?

Mild changes in memory speed are a widely recognised part of ageing, and most of the time they are not a cause for alarm. Even so, it is sensible to speak to your GP if forgetfulness is getting worse, is affecting daily life, or is worrying you or your family. Only a healthcare professional can tell you what is behind it.

What about the “Vicks trick”?

You may have seen the “Vicks trick” for memory shared online. It is important to be clear: chest rubs are made for temporary relief of cold symptoms, and there is no reliable scientific evidence that using one improves memory. We mention it here only because many people search for it, and we think it is better to look at what is actually being studied.

Blood flow and the ageing brain

The brain is a demanding organ. It makes up a small part of body weight but uses a large share of the body’s oxygen, which it receives through the blood. Studies have observed that blood flow to the brain tends to change as we age, and that the body’s natural production of nitric oxide, a molecule involved in relaxing blood vessels, may also decline over time.

This is an area of ongoing research, and it does not mean that any single product can change how your memory works. It does explain why so many people are interested in habits and nutrients that support healthy circulation.

Everyday habits that support brain health

  • Move regularly. Walking and other gentle activity support healthy circulation.
  • Protect your sleep. Rest plays a key role in how the brain organises the day’s information.
  • Stay socially and mentally active. Conversation, reading and hobbies keep the mind engaged.
  • Eat a balanced diet. Plenty of vegetables, fruit, oily fish, nuts and whole grains.
  • Keep up with check-ups. Blood pressure and general health matter for the brain too.
